# STV-M0-18: accounting drain and replay completion working design baseline

**Status: candidate implementation under review; not finally accepted.** This document defines #485's completion boundary using STV-M0-16 revision `7d2a54b03394be8d71c2018b73286e7a08a64cd5` ([#483 baseline selection](https://github.com/djh00t/steve/issues/483#issuecomment-5885737021)) and STV-M0-17 revision `48fc33f36d7a147db7373d30c9a5653749895f12` ([#484 baseline selection](https://github.com/djh00t/steve/issues/484#issuecomment-5885739296)) as working design baselines. The candidate durably publishes conservative `unclean` evidence before waits that may consume the single deadline, refreshes the strongest observed completion counts while time remains, and replaces that state with `reconciled` only after the named barriers verify. Final operator and release acceptance occurs once, at the combined touchable M1 release gate. These baselines and the candidate do not prove platform durability. #84 remains the coordination parent.

## Acknowledgement ladder

| Stage | Evidence | What it establishes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Accepted into the primary or journal channel | Send receipt tied to the full event ID and generation | Volatile ownership only. It is neither a durable journal entry nor a database outcome. |
| Journal written and flushed | Writer receipt with generation ID, record offset/length and content digest after a successful flush | The record reached the operating system's file buffers. It is not a durable-retention acknowledgement. |
| Journal synced | Same receipt after successful file sync, with a durable boundary covering the entire framed record; newly created file/name also needs the qualified directory sync | Durable-retention evidence only on a qualified OS/filesystem/storage path. A torn or unqualified record stays uncertain under #85. |
| Database committed | Transaction completion for a new row, or a committed read that verifies an existing row's full content | Reconciliation for that event under the database's configured durability contract. A connection error or unverified `ON CONFLICT DO NOTHING` is not this receipt. |
| Generation reconciled | Persisted replay manifest covers every complete journal record and every admitted volatile event with database receipts or truthful lost/unknown classifications; worker joins and final journal sync are recorded | This generation has no pending work silently discarded. It does not by itself close an incident. |
| Incident closed or acknowledged | Revision-checked, synced incident record covers the exact generation-state revision and journal digest tuples from #484 | `clear` requires complete reconciliation and no loss/unknown; `acknowledged` requires the authorized #483 disposition after the replay barrier. |

A receipt carries an event ID **and content identity**; an ID alone cannot reconstruct an event. `flush`, a successful channel send, `DatabasePool::insert_background_event` returning `Ok` today, and process exit do not advance the ladder beyond their actual evidence. No receipt is inferred from a counter or log line. A primary database write error first routes the full event to the monitored journal fallback. If the monitored journal fallback accepts the event, #483 remains `clear` while its writer works; fallback use alone does not stop inference or prove durable retention. A later journal write/flush/sync failure or both paths refusing responsibility latches `blocked` under the #483 working baseline. Report that failure promptly; already admitted requests may finish forwarding while later inference admission is stopped. A content conflict follows the proposed policy change below.

Database completion has exactly these outcomes:

| Outcome | Meaning | Acknowledges database completion |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `inserted` | The new row committed with the event's complete content. | Yes |
| `duplicate_identical` | A committed read in the replay transaction verified the existing row's `id`, `kind`, `payload`, and `created_at` against the complete journal record. | Yes |
| `duplicate_conflict` | The same ID has different content and the protected conflict snapshot is durable. | No; retain the journal and block closure. |
| `failed` | The operation definitively failed and evidence establishes that it did not commit. | No; retain and retry under #86. |
| `unknown` | Commit or read outcome is ambiguous or cannot be verified. | No; retain, reconnect, and verify under #86. |

Only `inserted` and `duplicate_identical` acknowledge database completion. A revision-checked receipt may advance from `failed` or `unknown` after a later verified attempt; it must never infer success from an ID, `Ok`, affected-row count, or channel receipt. #86 owns the configured per-operation timeout, total retry deadline, retry interval, exhaustion result, and startup/manual recovery trigger. #485 does not choose those values.

## STV-M0-16 working-baseline extension: replay content conflict

The STV-M0-16 working baseline includes `replay_content_conflict` in the public `accounting_incident.cause` enum and in the `blocked` transition. This remains candidate behavior for the single combined M1 release gate, not a separate approval request. After full-field verification and the protected conflict snapshot below are durable, detecting the same ID with different content blocks the logical owner: later inference receives #483's JSON `503 accounting_incident` error with `state: blocked`, `Cache-Control: no-store` and `Retry-After: 1`; `GET /health/ready` is `503 not_ready` with `accounting_incident.state: blocked` and `cause: replay_content_conflict`; status remains HTTP 200 with that incident object, liveness remains 200, and already admitted forwarding may finish. Preserve the original journal record, existing-row snapshot, conflict receipt and incident revision; public status redacts protected identities and evidence references. After restart an unresolved conflict remains `unreconciled`. It cannot become `clear` through automatic replay or `acknowledged` through loss/uncertainty disposition; only the verified offline recovery below can resolve it. Consumers must never silently map it to another cause or report successful replay.

## Durable evidence

Use the private root, installation identity, per-generation journal and coordination lock specified by the #484 working baseline. The following are **logical record shapes for implementation review**, not a schema migration or promise that these files exist. All persisted records need an explicit format version, integrity check, atomic publication/sync under the qualified platform contract, and a monotonically increasing revision. Missing, partial, corrupt, mismatched or unsupported evidence fails closed as `unreconciled`; it is never rebuilt as healthy from an empty journal.

| Record | Required fields and invariant |
| --- | --- |
| Generation state | `installation_id`, `generation_id`, `revision`, `phase` (`active`, `draining`, `unclean`, `reconciled`, `retired`), journal path plus byte length/content digest, last complete synced record boundary, admitted/worker-completed/journal-synced/DB-committed counts, replay manifest reference/digest, and last update time. Counts describe observed work, not inferred durability. A state revision advances whenever its journal or completion evidence changes, and is published under #484's coordination protocol. |
| Per-event replay receipt | `generation_id`, journal offset/length and record digest, `event_id`, full-content digest, outcome (`inserted`, `duplicate_identical`, `duplicate_conflict`, `failed`, `unknown`), database evidence reference and time when committed; a conflict also requires the protected conflict-snapshot reference/digest. The journal bytes remain the authoritative payload; a receipt is not a substitute. A receipt is append-only or revision checked so a crash cannot turn an unacknowledged outcome into success. |
| Replay manifest | Generation ID, frozen journal byte length/digest, complete record count, digest of ordered per-event receipts, count by outcome, parser result from #85, DB durability profile, and sync result. It covers a fixed snapshot, never a still-written journal. |
| Incident record and history | #483 state, incident ID/cause/counts, #484 coordination revision and exact coverage tuples `(generation_id, generation_state_revision, journal_evidence_digest)`, replay manifest references, and, for `acknowledged`, authenticated OS principal, UTC time, disposition kind and protected evidence reference. Prior incidents and dispositions remain auditable. Public status keeps actor and evidence reference redacted per #483. |
| Retirement marker | Generation ID, frozen journal digest, replay-manifest digest, incident closure/coverage revision or healthy no-incident evidence, and directory-sync result. A marker is a tombstone; it cannot replace the retained source until retention policy and audit requirements are met. |

The journal contains the full `id`, `kind`, `payload`, and `created_at` needed for replay. The retained source, receipts, manifest, generation state and incident history form one auditable chain. Do not store the event payload in public status. Missing worker receipt or uncertain admission count is `unknown`, never zero. #483's incident counts cover only incident-attributed payloads; healthy fallback backlog remains outside those counts until an incident opens.

## Ownership inputs consumed unchanged

Ordinary startup requires an explicitly provisioned absolute private root with a stable installation identity, coordination lock, revisioned incident evidence, and per-generation locked journals. First-install provisioning creates and syncs the initial evidence and manifest; startup never infers provisioning from an absent or empty path. Under the coordination lock, every update rereads the monotonic revision and covers exact `(generation_id, generation_state_revision, journal_evidence_digest)` tuples.

Poll complete incident and generation evidence every 50 ms, measure the 100 ms snapshot-freshness bound from poll start, and use a 100 ms coordination-lock acquisition timeout. An expired or incomplete snapshot, stale revision, missing/corrupt artifact, moved root, unsupported filesystem, or lock/read/write/sync/publication failure fails closed. A busy generation belongs to its writer or replayer and is not inspected. Cleanup is limited to an unlocked, zero-length unpublished staging file with no canonical journal or state record; everything else is retained and fails closed.

Legacy adoption is an explicit offline operation. The operator must externally stop every pre-protocol writer and disable its restart. `steve accounting adopt-legacy --source FILE --root PATH --maintenance-assertion FILE` requires an operator-supplied assertion naming the legacy source path, source host, assertion time, supervisor context, and claimed stop/disable action. The assertion is unauthenticated; its retained digest supports audit integrity only. The new coordination guard detects only cooperating protocol participants, so Steve cannot establish that all uncooperative old writers stopped, and guard success plus a source hash/length recheck cannot prove they will not append later. With the assertion retained, hash and sync an immutable backup, provision a distinct destination root, and import under a locked generation using #85 framing. An empty legacy source may complete vacuously. A nonempty source remains staged and pending, with the original and backup retained and ordinary startup failing closed, until #85 framing and #87 database acknowledgements complete under the Task 3 replay path. Only then sync an adoption marker, verify the original hash, move the source outside active discovery, and sync the directory. A missing assertion, missing migration evidence, or interrupted step fails closed and resumes from the backup; assertion presence does not verify the external stop claim. These requirements do not qualify a platform or power-loss behavior.

## Bounded drain and replacement

Management drain and process-signal shutdown enter the same accounting sequence and use one absolute deadline calculated once. Management drain preserves its existing non-terminating behavior: it remains running and not-ready after success, and on timeout keeps ownership while any writer remains alive. Signal shutdown exits after the same barriers; its supervisor enforces the outer termination bound.

1. On shutdown, close **new inference admission** with the existing lifecycle behavior, choose one monotonic deadline using the configured drain timeout, and publish `draining` with its revision. Keep accounting acceptance open to tracked requests already admitted before that cutoff. Let those producers finish forwarding and emit their accounting event, or reach the shared deadline; only then close accounting acceptance for this generation. A request still held at the deadline makes completion uncertain and forces the non-success path below. Healthy replacement can already be ready on its own locked generation; it must not wait for old-process exit.
2. Keep explicit completion handles for the primary accounting worker and journal writer. After admitted producers quiesce and accounting acceptance closes, drain accepted primary items to verified database outcomes, route DB failures to the journal, then drain journal-channel items through the **synced** boundary. A worker's exit receipt includes its accepted/completed/fallback/uncertain counts and final error. Join both handles; channel closure or `inflight == 0` is not a join. History and telemetry can follow their own lifecycle rules and do not assert accounting completion.
3. Before the deadline, freeze the journal under its generation lock, sync it, reconcile its complete records and any tracked volatile items, and persist a replay manifest. Publish `reconciled` and its exact coverage only when all named receipts verify. Release the generation lock after this evidence is durable. The successor may inspect the unlocked generation under #484's lock/snapshot rules and may retire it only through the ordering below.
4. At the deadline, do not wait without bound for a stuck writer or database. Publish `unclean` plus the strongest truthful worker/journal/incident evidence that can be synced, keep the incident blocked or unreconciled, and return an explicit non-success drain result. Management drain remains running and not-ready and keeps the generation lock while any writer is alive. Signal shutdown retains ownership until its writers join or process termination releases the OS lock; the supervisor enforces the outer kill bound. If the evidence write itself fails or the process is forcibly killed, the last durable `active`/`draining` record and retained journal are the evidence; the next owner must not infer a clean drain from lock release, empty bytes or exit code. The current `server::serve_until_drained` timeout covers request in-flight work only and is not an accounting-worker deadline.

A still-live predecessor holds its own journal lock; replacement never reads or truncates it. If it dies, the successor acquires the now-free lock, verifies the persisted generation and incident evidence, and treats incomplete/unclean or uncertain volatile work as `unreconciled`. A healthy overlap is not itself an incident. Concurrent failure publication or a new/changed uncovered generation tuple wins over any stale closure attempt through #484's coordination revision check.

A successful drain record contains the generation and incident revisions, single deadline and completion time, admitted/completed/fallback/uncertain counts, joined worker identities/results, final journal synced boundary/digest, replay-manifest digest, coverage tuples, and directory-sync result. A timed-out drain records the same deadline, incomplete stages, strongest known counts/errors and retained-artifact references; it remains `unclean` or `unreconciled`. If that record cannot be synced, the pre-persisted `active`/`draining` state remains the conservative restart evidence.

## Replay, retirement and incident closure

For each complete record in an unlocked, frozen generation, perform an idempotent **content-verified** database operation on `(id, kind, payload, created_at)`. A new ID is `inserted` only after the insert transaction commits. For an existing ID, read the stored `kind`, `payload`, and `created_at` in the same transaction/isolation boundary and compare with the complete source values; exact equality is `duplicate_identical`, and any difference is `duplicate_conflict`. Serialize the source JSON payload consistently for comparison, or compare a documented canonical representation on both sides. A digest can speed lookup but cannot replace full-field verification. A definitive operation error is `failed`. If commit/read outcome is ambiguous, reconnect and verify the row; retain `unknown` until verified. #87 must replace both backends' current `ON CONFLICT(id) DO NOTHING` success ambiguity with this outcome contract.

`inserted` and `duplicate_identical` each count as one reconciled event, never two. A conflicting duplicate is unresolved data-integrity evidence; its incident transition is the amendment above. Within the transaction that reads the existing row, capture its full `(id, kind, payload, created_at)` values and a verification ID; persist an immutable protected conflict snapshot containing those values, their digest, the source journal record digest, database/backend and transaction isolation, and read evidence. Bind its durable reference/digest to the replay receipt before reporting `duplicate_conflict`. If this snapshot cannot be persisted, retain `failed` or `unknown` and the journal; never claim a verified conflict from an unbound later read. Do not overwrite the row, mark replay complete, or permit automatic retirement or incident closure. Retry does not make a conflict identical. Database outage, retry exhaustion, malformed framing and uncertain commit likewise retain the journal and receipts. #85 owns torn-tail/framing classification; #86 owns bounded retry timing and retained-path behavior at exhaustion. Neither may reclassify an unverified record as success.

### Offline conflict inspection and recovery

`steve accounting audit --root PATH --incident ID --revision N` is a private, read-only command. It takes the coordination lock, verifies the requested revision, and displays the exact journal record, existing-row snapshot, protected conflict digest/reference, backend/isolation evidence and coverage tuples. It requires local read permission on the private root and is never exposed through HTTP.

Recovery requires every serving/replay process for the installation to be stopped. While holding the coordination and affected generation locks, run `steve accounting resolve-conflict --root PATH --incident ID --revision N --event-id ID --authoritative journal|database --evidence-ref REF`. The command derives its actor from the authenticated OS process principal and accepts no actor text. It verifies both stored conflict sides and their digests against the named revision before changing anything. With `journal`, it conditionally replaces the database row only while the conflicting preimage still matches, commits, rereads and verifies all fields. With `database`, it verifies the committed row and records the retained journal frame as superseded without altering its bytes. Both paths sync an immutable resolution record containing the preimages, selected authority, final content digest, OS principal, time, evidence reference and new coverage revision, then reread the final database and evidence state. Any active writer, stale revision, digest mismatch, write/sync error or failed reread leaves the incident blocked/unreconciled and preserves both sides.

`steve accounting acknowledge` cannot resolve `replay_content_conflict`; accepted loss or uncertainty is not a bypass. Only the verified offline resolution above may remove the conflict outcome, after which the ordinary replay barrier and revision-checked closure rules apply.

After #85 accounts for every byte of the frozen journal and **every** replayable record has a verified DB outcome with no `duplicate_conflict`, `failed`, or `unknown` item, sync the manifest, publish `reconciled`, and verify its frozen journal length/digest again. A torn or unclassified tail prevents this transition; #85 must first supply its classified disposition and retained evidence for gate review. Under the coordination lock, reread the current incident revision and all covered generation tuples. If no incident exists, record affirmative healthy/no-incident coverage; if an incident exists, apply #483's exact accounting categories. Return it to `clear` only when all retained work is reconciled, volatile work is accounted for, and no confirmed loss or provisional uncertainty remains. Where eligible loss or irreducible uncertainty remains, require the authorized, durable #483 disposition to enter visibly `acknowledged`; its pending replay counts must be zero. The offline command is `steve accounting acknowledge --root PATH --incident ID --revision N --kind KIND --evidence-ref REF`; it derives actor from the authenticated OS process principal (effective UID/account on Unix; process-token SID/account on Windows), accepts no `--actor`, and ignores identity environment variables. The protected audit record contains incident revision, disposition kind, derived actor, UTC time, protected evidence reference, and exact coverage tuples. `steve accounting audit --root PATH --incident ID --revision N` reads that protected record without mutation; public health and status always render actor and evidence reference as null. Publish/sync the closure or disposition before admission reopens; verified records restore after restart. A conflict is ineligible for disposition. A stale snapshot, uncovered or changed tuple, missing manifest, or failed sync keeps the gate closed. A later failure opens a new incident without erasing prior audit history.

Only then publish/sync a retirement marker for a reconciled generation. Move its journal outside active discovery and sync the directory; retain the source bytes, receipts, manifest, marker and incident history for the configured audit/backup retention period. Deletion is a separate retention operation, never a drain/replay side effect. A crash between any marker, move or directory sync step leaves enough source and marker evidence to recheck and resume idempotently; startup fails closed if their relation is ambiguous. The offline legacy adoption marker in #484 uses the same verified replay acknowledgement and keeps its original source and backup.

## Failure examples and limits

- **Success:** a primary worker's database transaction commits and its handle joins; a fallback event has a complete synced journal receipt, then replay inserts it. The frozen manifest, generation state and incident coverage sync. Only then is the generation reconciled and eligible for retirement.
- **Success:** replay meets an existing ID with identical `kind`, serialized payload and `created_at`; a committed read returns `duplicate_identical`. The event is reconciled once without a second insert.
- **Success:** an already admitted request is held while shutdown closes new inference admission. It finishes forwarding and emits E before the shared deadline; accounting acceptance then closes, E obtains a DB or synced-journal receipt, and both workers join. Closing the accounting channel when shutdown first starts would fail this case.
- **Failure:** the DB blocks while the writer has only flushed its bytes. The deadline expires. Drain reports non-success, retains the journal and incomplete generation state, and restart reports `unreconciled`; it cannot report durable replay or `clear`.
- **Failure:** the writer syncs a record, but its worker never joins. Durable replayability may be known for that record; missing volatile-completion evidence prevents a clean generation and forces reconciliation or truthful unknown classification.
- **Failure:** the ID exists with a different payload or timestamp. `ON CONFLICT DO NOTHING`, row count zero, or an `Ok` result must not count as successful replay. Retain both evidence sides and block closure.
- **Failure:** a crash after a DB commit but before its receipt is synced may replay the event. The row's full content determines `duplicate_identical` or `duplicate_conflict`. A crash before DB commit leaves `failed` or `unknown` until read verification; a crash during journal append delegates record framing to #85. Neither crash may erase the journal automatically.
- **Failure:** a closure snapshot covers revision 4, but another process publishes a failure at revision 5. The revision-4 clear/acknowledgement is rejected and admission remains blocked. Healthy old/new overlap with unchanged, verified evidence does not itself force failure.

These are logical ordering claims. `flush` is not sync; a successful sync or DB commit supports only the guarantees of its named, qualified OS/filesystem/storage and database configuration. Power-loss survival, cross-host recovery and unqualified filesystems are **not** claimed by this baseline. A process crash can be exercised in CI; actual power-loss behavior needs separate platform/storage qualification under #484.
